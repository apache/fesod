Anyone being supportive of the community and working in any of the CoPDoC areas can become an Apache Fesod PMC member. The CoPDoC is an acronym from ASF to describe how we recognize your contributions not only by code.

- **(Co)mmunity** - You can join us via our mailing list, issue trackers, discussions page to interact with community members, and share vision and knowledge
- **(P)roject** - a clear vision and consensus are needed
- **(Do)cumentation** - without it, the stuff remains only in the minds of the authors
- **(C)ode** - discussion goes nowhere without code

The Apache Fesod community strives to be meritocratic. Thus, once someone has contributed sufficiently to any area of CoPDoC they can be a candidate for PMC membership and at last voted in as an Apache Fesod PMC member. Being an Apache Fesod PMC member does not necessarily mean you must commit code with your commit privilege to the codebase; it means you are committed to the Apache Fesod project and are productively contributing to our community's success.

## PMC member requirements

There are no strict rules for becoming a committer or PMC member. Candidates for new PMC members are typically people that are active contributors and community members. Anyway, if the rules can be clarified a little bit, it can somehow clear the doubts in the minds of contributors and make the community more transparent, reasonable, and fair.

### Continuous contributions

PMC member candidates should have a decent amount of continuous engagements and contributions (fixing bugs, adding new features, writing documentation, maintaining issues boards, code review, or answering community questions) to Apache Fesod.

- 12+ months with activity and engagement.

### Quality of contributions

- A solid general understanding of the project
- Well tested, well-designed, following Apache Fesod coding standards, and simple patches.
- Well-organized and detailed user-oriented documentation.

### Community involvement

- Be active, courteous, and respectful on the dev mailing list and help mentor newer contributors and users.
- Be active, courteous, and respectful on the issue tracker for project maintenance
- Be active, courteous, and respectful for pull requests reviewing
- Be involved in the design road map discussions with a professional and diplomatic approach even if there is a disagreement
- Promoting the project by writing articles or holding events

## How PMC membership is granted

PMC membership is decided by the PMC itself, through a vote on the private mailing list following the [ASF voting rules](https://www.apache.org/foundation/voting.html). The [Apache PMC guide](https://www.apache.org/dev/pmc.html) describes the responsibilities of PMC members and the roster process in more detail.

1. **Propose a candidate.** Any PMC member can start a `[DISCUSS]` thread on <private@fesod.apache.org> proposing a contributor for PMC membership. Use the candidate's full public name rather than only a GitHub id, and summarize their contributions across the CoPDoC areas.
2. **Vote.** When the discussion shows support from a majority of those responding, start a separate `[VOTE]` thread on <private@fesod.apache.org> and let it run for one week. A positive result requires at least three +1 binding votes and no -1 vetoes.
3. **Record the result and invite.** Post the outcome to the same list with a `[RESULT] [VOTE]` subject, then invite the candidate privately, copying <private@fesod.apache.org>. The candidate may decline in private.
4. **Update the roster.** After the candidate accepts, the PMC chair adds the new member to the PMC roster with the [Whimsy roster tool](https://whimsy.apache.org/roster/committee/fesod), which also notifies the Board automatically. The new member is announced on <dev@fesod.apache.org> and is expected to subscribe to <private@fesod.apache.org>.

If the candidate is not already a committer, the PMC grants committer privileges first; see [Nominate Committer](./nominate-committer.md) and [Onboarding](./onboarding.md) for the next steps. The PMC chair is an ASF officer appointed by the Board and reports on the project's health to the Board on a quarterly basis.
